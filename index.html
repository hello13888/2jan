Do I need a visa for mainland China?

Checked 25 November 2026. Rules can change, so please confirm for your own passport.

Passports with 30-day visa-free entry

China lets ordinary passport holders from 48 countries enter visa-free for up to 30 days. The list includes the UK, most of Europe, Australia, New Zealand, Canada, Japan and South Korea.

This arrangement is confirmed only until 31 December 2026. An extension into 2027 has not been announced yet. The wedding is on 2 January.

What we suggest: file a visa application now at visaforchina.cn. Filing is free. If visa-free entry is renewed you can ignore it. If it is not, you visit the visa centre and pay the fee, with most of the work already done.

If you enter mainland China on or before 31 December, your stay is covered by the current rules. That does not work if you are joining us in Hong Kong that evening.

Other passports

If your passport is not on the visa-free list (we understand this includes US passports), you need a visa. Please apply as early as you can.

The 240-hour transit rule

China also allows 55 nationalities, including the US and UK, to stay up to 10 days without a visa when in transit. It needs a confirmed onward ticket from the mainland to a different country or region than the one you arrived from.

It does not cover Hong Kong → Shenzhen → Hong Kong, which is what most guests will do. It can work if you leave the mainland to somewhere else, for example flying home directly from Shenzhen or Guangzhou. Check the entry port rules before relying on it.

Hong Kong residents

  • Chinese-national permanent residents: bring your Home Return Permit 回鄉證 and your HKID. New permits and renewals go through China Travel Service, take about 12 working days and cost HK$390.
  • Non-Chinese permanent residents: you can use the Mainland Travel Permit for Hong Kong and Macao Residents (Non-Chinese Citizens), also issued through China Travel Service. Without it, follow the rules for your passport above.
  • Other Hong Kong residents: follow the rules for your passport.

Whatever you hold, check it is valid on 2 January.

Crossing the border yourself

The hotel is about 10 minutes by taxi from the border.

  • MTR: East Rail Line to Lok Ma Chau, cross at Futian Port.
  • Bus: cross-border coaches run to Huanggang Port, which reopened in a new building in October 2026 and is open 24 hours.
  • High speed rail: Hong Kong West Kowloon to Shenzhen Futian, about 15 minutes.
  • Flying: the nearest airports are Shenzhen Bao'an and Hong Kong International.

Phone, internet and apps

WhatsApp, Instagram, YouTube and Google do not work on mainland Wi-Fi or local SIMs. An international eSIM with roaming data gets around this. On hotel Wi-Fi the blocks still apply.

One option: a 45 GB data-only eSIM for mainland China and Hong Kong, about £20, from CUniq.

Install before you arrive

  • Amap 高德地图: maps, and you can call a taxi in it
  • Alipay or WeChat 微信: China is close to cashless. Both accept foreign bank cards.
  • Didi: taxis, which are cheap
  • Dianping or Meituan: restaurants, massages, KTV and other things to do

The Shenzhen Metro takes Alipay once it is set up.
